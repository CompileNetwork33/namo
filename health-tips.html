Never Skip a Dose — But Know When to Skip Safely

Missing a dose of medication can disrupt your treatment. However, if you miss a dose, what you should do depends entirely on the medicine and how much time has passed. Never double-dose without consulting your pharmacist — some medicines like blood thinners can be dangerous if doubled, while others are safe to take as soon as you remember. Always keep your medicines at the same time each day using an alarm or a pill organiser.

How to Store Medicines Correctly at Home

Heat, light, and moisture can degrade medicines. Keep them in a cool, dry place — not the bathroom. Always check expiry dates, store insulin in the refrigerator, and keep medicines out of children's reach.

Monsoon Health Guide: Staying Safe This Season

Monsoon brings an increased risk of dengue, malaria, cholera, and fungal infections. Use mosquito repellents, drink boiled or filtered water, avoid street food, and keep ORS sachets at home.

Monitor your blood sugar at consistent times, carry glucose tablets for emergencies, rotate insulin injection sites, and never skip meals when on insulin. A glucometer calibration check every 6 months is essential.

Fever in Children: When to Worry and What to Do

A fever above 38°C in infants under 3 months needs immediate medical attention. For older children, paracetamol syrup every 4–6 hours (correct dosage by weight), plenty of fluids, and sponging with lukewarm water can help.

The Importance of Hydration — Beyond Just Water

Dehydration affects kidney function, blood pressure, and cognitive performance. Aim for 8–10 glasses daily, increase intake during illness, and include coconut water or ORS during heavy sweating. Urine colour is the best hydration indicator.

Understanding Antibiotics: Use Them Right

Antibiotics only work against bacteria — not viruses like the common cold. Always complete the full course even if you feel better, take them at the same time daily, and never share with others. Antibiotic resistance is a growing global health crisis.

Blood Pressure: Simple Lifestyle Habits That Work

Reduce sodium intake, walk 30 minutes daily, quit smoking, limit alcohol, and take BP medicines consistently — even when readings seem normal. Monitor at home with a validated device and log readings to share with your doctor.

Sleep & Your Health: Why 7–8 Hours Matters

Chronic sleep deprivation raises the risk of obesity, diabetes, heart disease, and depression. Stick to consistent sleep and wake times, avoid screens 1 hour before bed, and keep your bedroom cool and dark for the best rest.
